# Community and patient-organisation sources for the rare-disease KG

Research snapshot: 2026-10-03. This is an extraction-planning note, not permission to republish source content. **Documented** means an official source page states it; **live sample** means fields/links were visible on a currently served page; **inference** is a suggested modelling or operational choice. No source-specific public machine-readable API was confirmed during this review; that is not proof that no API/export exists. Recheck source terms, robots policy, and contact/data access conditions before any recurring bulk collection.

## NORD (US National Organization for Rare Disorders)

**Source and evidence.** NORD's [rare disease A–Z database](https://rarediseases.org/rare-diseases/) says it is an ongoing project based on established rare-disease ontologies and collaboration with MONDO, Orphanet, and OMIM. Search/list entry pages link to disease reports. A live [Trismus-pseudocamptodactyly syndrome report](https://rarediseases.org/rare-diseases/trismus-pseudocamptodactyly-syndrome/) showed a stable slug URL, display name, last-updated date, prior publication years, summary, synonym list, report sections, and “Patient Organizations” entries. Those entries included organization name, NORD-member marker, contact fields, related rare diseases, and organization profile URL. The [organization index](https://rarediseases.org/organizations/) paginates and identifies itself as NORD's Organizational Database; its page says organizations interested in listing may contact NORD. NORD also provides an explicit [request form for a subset of the rare-disease database as CSV](https://rarediseases.org/request-rdb-access/); it asks for name, email, phone, user type, intended use, and acceptance of terms. This is a gated request, not an open download/API, and its page does not specify which columns are supplied. Do not assume the CSV includes patient-organization associations; it is explicitly a subset of the disease database.

**Extraction path.** Prefer requesting the CSV subset through that official form, and only ingest fields expressly covered by the grant and supplied terms. This export is described as a disease-database subset; it is not documented as an organization directory, so do not assume it includes patient-organization edges. NORD's current terms prohibit scraping/copying and reserve reuse rights; absent a permission compatible with the KG, stop at linked citations and manual schema research rather than extracting source content. Capture disease pages via their canonical page URLs and organization profiles via profile URLs. The site uses pagination for the organization index; follow visible next-page links rather than assuming page counts/offset parameters. No documented public API was confirmed. A single read-only request to `https://rarediseases.org/robots.txt` returned 200 `text/plain`; it allows `/` to all user agents, sets a 10-second crawl delay, and disallows specified administrative/private paths and search-query pages. This is a crawl directive, not a reuse grant.

**KG mapping (inference).** Nodes: `Disease` (source=NORD, source ID=canonical slug/URL), `Organization` (source=NORD ODB; profile slug/URL), optionally `DiseaseName`/`Synonym`. Fields: disease preferred label, exact source synonyms, NORD report URL, last-updated date, source publication years; organization display name, profile URL, NORD-member flag, contact points only if necessary and permitted. Edges: `HAS_PATIENT_ORGANIZATION` from condition page's explicit “related rare diseases” membership; `HAS_SYNONYM` with provenance; `MEMBER_OF_NORD` only when the source displays its NORD-member marker. Use NORD slug as local key and map disease identity to MONDO/Orphanet/OMIM identifiers only from explicit, validated mappings; do not infer exact disease equivalence from text similarity. Keep each disease-organization assertion's originating page and capture date.

**Updates, quality, rights.** Revisit index pagination and known URLs periodically, detect changes by last-updated and content hash, and retain an observation history rather than overwriting changed relationships. Page text is editorial, reports can be old, and the database says it is still being expanded: missing record/organization does not mean disease has no community. Synonyms may be legacy, overlapping, or not one-to-one; related organizations can be connected to several diseases. NORD's [site terms](https://rarediseases.org/privacy/) and site footer state that site content is proprietary, personal/non-commercial viewing is limited, and copying/scraping/reuse needs prior written permission. The request CSV has its own Terms of Use acceptance. Public web visibility is not a bulk-reuse license. Request permission for the intended derived/indexed use and review the supplied terms before extraction. The NORD robots.txt request was successful and its directives are recorded above.

## Global Genes (US-based/global advocacy and support network)

**Source and evidence.** Global Genes now calls its patient-organization community the [Global Advocacy Alliance](https://globalgenes.org/about-us/global-advocacy-alliance/), formerly the RARE Foundation Alliance. The current official page describes 860+ organizations, 46 countries, and 1,000+ diseases represented; it offers a **View Full Member List** link at `/about-us/global-advocacy-alliance/global-advocacy-alliance-members/`. A single read-only fetch of that list URL returned HTTP 403 (as did the PDF route below); search indexing exposes the page route but not list contents in the HTML-readable view used here. An official [2022 member-list PDF](https://globalgenes.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/RARE-Foundation-Alliance-List-RARE-Hub-2022-03.pdf) appears in search results and names organizations paired with disease labels; an older [2019 list PDF](https://globalgenes.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/RARE-Foundation-Alliance-List.pdf) says 500+ organizations. These are concrete historical snapshots, not current memberships. The current alliance page establishes that member organizations are listed on Global Genes' website and refreshed contact information is requested annually, but no machine-readable export/API was confirmed. The RARE List is a separate disease-information product, not a member directory.

Do not substitute Genetic Alliance's Disease InfoSearch for Global Genes. The latter belongs to Genetic Alliance US. Global Genes' [RARE Concierge resource hub](https://globalgenes.org/rare-disease-patient-services/) is curated patient guidance and links to an externally hosted trial search; it is not the primary organization list. The separate [Corporate Alliance Directory](https://globalgenes.org/about-us/rare-corporate-alliance/) is member-only.

**Extraction path.** The best source route is the official Global Advocacy Alliance member list. Because its public endpoint returned 403 during this review, do not retry around the block, emulate a browser, or fetch hidden data. The official historical PDFs show a concrete schema (organization name plus one or more disease labels), but they are dated snapshots and their direct PDF route returned 403 in this pass. Do not bypass that response or treat indexed snippets as a usable export; seek Global Genes permission and an accessible current list. For current membership, rely on the visible aggregate figures until Global Genes provides accessible list data or written permission/access. No public CSV/API was confirmed. Do not automate membership forms, gated areas, patient-service forms, or third-party trials tools.

**KG mapping (inference).** The historical list's directly supported fields are organization name and listed disease label(s); country and current contact/activity were not demonstrated in its entries sampled from search indexing. If Global Genes later supplies an authorized current list, model `Organization` with source ID `global-genes-gaa:<normalized name or supplied ID>`, literal display name, listed condition string(s), list version/date, and source URL. Link conditions only after terminology review; preserve the raw label for many-to-many or ambiguous entries. Add `MEMBER_OF(Global Advocacy Alliance)` with valid-time/capture-time evidence. The public GAA page says members are members for life and asks for annual contact updates, so do not use stale contact details as current and do not infer that no longer-mentioned entries left the alliance. Keep source assertions separate when reconciling against other directories.

**Updates, quality, rights.** Alliance membership is time-dependent and eligibility includes support groups, US 501(c)(3) foundations, and international registered charities providing rare-disease support, education, awareness/advocacy, or research. Do not infer current member status from the dated PDFs; a historical record can support only `MEMBER_OF(Global Advocacy Alliance)` during the stated snapshot, not current membership. Map condition links exactly as listed in source, preserving ambiguity where labels bundle more than one disease. Current Global Genes [Terms of Use](https://globalgenes.org/globalgenes-rare-x-tou/) prohibit collecting/using site content and data-mining, robots, or similar extraction methods; therefore even manual transcription for this KG needs explicit permission under the requested use, regardless of whether a PDF is indexed. A single `robots.txt` request returned HTTP 403, so no robots directives could be read. The current member-list URL and PDF also returned 403 in one direct read attempt; record this as access failure, not a basis to bypass. Bulk extraction therefore requires explicit permission/access. RARE Concierge content is patient-oriented guidance and carries an explicit statement that it is general knowledge, not medical advice.

## EURORDIS – Rare Diseases Europe

**Source and evidence.** EURORDIS is a European alliance of patient organizations. The official [Our Members directory](https://www.eurordis.org/who-we-are/our-members/) is referenced from official EURORDIS event registration instructions as the place to search for member organizations; individual organization profiles are served under `/members/` (example: [Czech Huntington Association](https://www.eurordis.org/members/spolecnost-pro-pomoc-pri-huntingtonove-chorobe-z-s-czech-huntington-association/)). EURORDIS reports membership totals and separately describes outreach/contact records for a larger population of European patient groups; membership must therefore not be conflated with all known organizations. Search indexing and the official page route establish public HTML profiles, but a live directory-page fetch returned HTTP 429 once, while the official individual profile page was discoverable. No public API or CSV export was confirmed.

**Extraction path.** Start from the official member directory and its profile links. For public, accessible records, human-curate or use conservative HTML extraction of profile pages, retaining the directory's pagination/filter state if present. Capture membership relation and profile data only for entries visible without login. Do not harvest participant registries or member-only contact databases. During this pass, one directory fetch returned HTTP 429 and one `https://www.eurordis.org/robots.txt` request returned 404; a request to `/privacy-policy/` returned 200, and a guessed `/legal-notice` route returned 404. The directory's HTML was not inspected deeply enough to claim its exact fields/pagination. A single 429 is a reason to pause and seek a permitted route, not to retry aggressively. No dedicated terms or content-reuse license was found in this review; the privacy policy is not a reuse license.

**KG mapping (inference).** Model `Organization` with source namespace `eurordis_member`, canonical EURORDIS profile path as source key, official/display name, country/geographic coverage, profile URL, and membership status/observation date when present. Link to `Disease` only when the profile or associated official content explicitly identifies represented condition(s); national umbrella/federation organizations may map to multiple conditions or none. Edges: `MEMBER_OF(EURORDIS)`, `REPRESENTS(Disease)` with quoted/structured source evidence, and `LOCATED_IN(Country)` when stated. Preserve language-specific and historical names as aliases rather than merging solely on name similarity.

**Updates, quality, rights.** Reconcile directory changes periodically and treat membership as time-dependent. EURORDIS membership is selective and does not represent all European patient groups; older reports give historical counts, so use directory observations for current membership. Network structures include national alliances and federations that may represent many disease organizations. Terms/licensing and API/export availability were not verified; do not assume the list is open data. Confirm reuse rights with EURORDIS before bulk collection and retain source profile links.

## Rare Disease UK (RDUK)

**Source and evidence.** Rare Disease UK is a national campaign run by [Genetic Alliance UK](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/), which describes RDUK as its campaign and voice for the UK rare-disease community. The RDUK site ([raredisease.org.uk](https://raredisease.org.uk/)) is principally campaign, policy, and community-experience content in this review, not a searchable disease/support-group directory. Genetic Alliance UK's official [member A–Z directory](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/membership/a-z-members-directory/) is the concrete public directory to use for member organizations. Its live HTML page labels itself as an alphabetical list of all member organizations and exposes names linked to member websites. It has alphabet jump links rather than an exposed public data export in the page inspected. No RDUK public API/export was confirmed.

**Extraction path.** Do not treat RDUK's campaign site as a condition catalogue. For an RDUK-derived KG, curate official campaign claims, publications, named partner/member mentions, and policy relations as separate evidence records. For community organization coverage use Genetic Alliance UK's member A–Z as a separate source (see next section): read its alphabet sections and outbound links, preserving the distinction between RDUK (campaign) and GAUK (alliance/member directory). No need to crawl individual member websites unless independently authorized and required.

**KG mapping (inference).** Create a `Campaign`/`Organization` node for Rare Disease UK with `RUN_BY` Genetic Alliance UK. Model reports, policy campaigns, and participation/evidence outputs as `Resource` or `CampaignActivity` nodes, each linked to its canonical URL and date. Avoid creating disease-organization edges from broad campaign content unless the named link explicitly establishes representation. RDUK is not itself equivalent to the member list or the UK rare-disease community as a whole.

**Updates, quality, rights.** Campaign pages and policy positions are time-specific; distinguish present campaign status from historical publications and quote dates. One read-only request to `https://raredisease.org.uk/robots.txt` returned 200 HTML after redirecting to Genetic Alliance UK's RDUK campaign page; it did not return a robots file, confirming that the old domain currently redirects rather than supplying separate robots directives. The Genetic Alliance UK `/robots.txt` endpoint returned 200 plain text, allowing general crawling except `/wp-admin/`. Genetic Alliance UK's [citations policy](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/citations-policy/) expressly permits reproduction of original, unmodified site material for non-commercial use with attribution; it does not explicitly settle bulk extraction or transformed KG reuse, so seek clarification for those cases. The [privacy policy](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/privacy-policy/) covers personal data and is not itself a content license. For member entries, official inclusion signals membership at observation time, not completeness across all UK disease communities. Confirm terms and refresh policy with Genetic Alliance UK for recurring reuse.

## Genetic Alliance (United States) and Genetic Alliance UK (distinct entities)

**Disambiguation.** This section covers both because names are easily conflated. **Genetic Alliance** at [geneticalliance.org](https://geneticalliance.org/) is a US 501(c)(3); it operates Disease InfoSearch and research infrastructure. **Genetic Alliance UK** at [geneticalliance.org.uk](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/) is a UK alliance of charities/support groups and runs the Rare Disease UK campaign. They are separate organizations; never merge based on the shared name. Stable source IDs must include the domain/namespace (`geneticalliance-us:*` versus `geneticalliance-uk:*`).

### Genetic Alliance US / Disease InfoSearch

The official [Disease InfoSearch page](https://geneticalliance.org/disease-info-search) describes a directory of 10,000+ diseases and 13,000+ conditions with curated information from advocacy organizations, support groups, and foundations; it says organizations can submit/create listings. Historical Genetic Alliance annual reporting describes algorithmically assembled condition information from public databases combined with material reviewed by advocacy organizations. The live promotional page links to the separate Disease InfoSearch app. A read-only visit to its Diseases and Support Groups routes showed search/letter controls but returned “No diseases found” and “No support groups found” in the rendered snapshot; that is an observed live sample, not evidence the underlying directory is empty. No result record was accessible through the page view, and no open download/API was documented. A single `robots.txt` request returned HTTP 200 HTML (a Next.js not-found page), not a robots directive. The app states: “Permission is granted under the copyright for educational, non-commercial use”; other uses require copyright-holder permission. Genetic Alliance's main site separately states CC BY 3.0 except where noted, so do not apply that broad site notice to the separately hosted Disease InfoSearch app. For this KG, first seek authorized data access; absent that, human-curate a small number of publicly rendered results only after resolving the app's exact license/terms with the rights holder. Avoid crawling registries or participant-level data: Genetic Alliance runs separate registry/biobank services, which are not public directory data.

Map condition records as `DiseaseInfoSearchCondition` keyed by source URL or source-native ID if supplied; capture preferred label, linked advocacy/support organizations, resource category, external URL, and any source-controlled curation date. Link condition nodes to canonical disease concepts only with reviewed mappings. Map organization listings as `SUPPORTED_BY`/`HAS_RESOURCE` relationships and mark them `curated_by_advocacy_org` only when the page supports that claim. Keep out-of-date or unavailable listings as status assertions with observation dates; recheck by link status and source changes. Quality pitfalls: the source spans common and rare conditions, conditions and subtypes can overlap, and user-submitted organization entries do not establish current activity. Disease InfoSearch has an explicit educational/non-commercial notice and requires permission for other uses. A low-volume check of the main Genetic Alliance robots route returned HTTP 200 `text/html` containing a Next.js not-found page, not a valid robots file; the guessed `/terms-of-use` route returned an app page without being reachable as a terms document. Treat its API/export and extraction authorization as unresolved and seek written permission before bulk use.

### Genetic Alliance UK / member organizations

The official [membership A–Z](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/membership/a-z-members-directory/) is an alphabetized public list. In the current live page, every observed entry has a display name and outbound website link. The page itself says it lists all member organizations and invites users seeking support for an unlisted condition to contact the alliance. This supports organization-level harvesting with a reasonable public HTML/human curation workflow; it does not expose condition tags, geographic remit, contact details, or stable member identifiers for every item in the list. No API/export was confirmed. The directory is alphabetically sectioned and exposes A–Z anchor links; pagination was not visible in the inspected page.

Capture source display name, outbound URL as listed, canonical directory URL, initial letter/section, and capture date. Use canonical organization domain (normalized, retaining original URL) as a provisional local identity key; where there is no domain, use source URL plus a generated local ID, and reconcile aliases manually. Only attach conditions after checking the member's own official statement or a trusted terminology source. Edges: `MEMBER_OF(Genetic Alliance UK)` and, with direct evidence, `SUPPORTS(Disease)`; store provenance separately for each edge. Quality pitfalls: umbrella charities can support several conditions; some entries are broad services or networks rather than disease-specific groups; two separate listings can share a name/domain or have outdated outbound links. The directory includes former names in some display labels, so retain aliases and avoid treating rebranding as two organizations.

One read-only check of Genetic Alliance UK's robots file returned 200 `text/plain`, with `User-agent: *`, disallowing `/wp-admin/` and allowing `/wp-admin/admin-ajax.php`; it exposes a sitemap. Its [citations policy](https://geneticalliance.org.uk/citations-policy/) permits non-commercial reproduction of original, unmodified website material with attribution, but says nothing explicit about bulk extraction or converted KG data. Use a small attributed seed within that permission, and obtain clarification for recurring/bulk transformed reuse. No API/export was confirmed. Directory membership indicates alliance membership, not exhaustive UK patient-organization coverage and not necessarily a disease relation.

## Shared KG conventions

These source lists describe organizations and their relationships, not a complete census of rare diseases or patient communities. Keep source-native identity and claims intact: source IDs/URLs, observation time, exact label, language, relationship wording, and evidence locator. Resolve disease concepts against an authoritative disease terminology separately; never use fuzzy name match as a merge decision. Represent membership, representation, support provision, and mere mention as different predicates. Store status temporally (active/closed/unknown only when evidence exists); a dead link alone is not enough to declare an organization closed. Preserve conflicting organization names, disease mappings, geography, and membership claims as sourced assertions for review.
