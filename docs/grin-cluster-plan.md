# GRIN research connections: cluster decision and demo plan

Decision recorded: 3 October 2026. **Agreed direction:** begin with GRIN2A- and GRIN2B-related neurodevelopmental disorders, focusing on variants with published functional evidence supporting reduced NMDA-receptor function.

This is the plan for our first real-data cluster. It does not mean the biomedical dataset, expert review or frontend integration is complete. The existing runnable atlas uses fictional acceptance fixtures; those must remain clearly distinguished from this research dataset.

## 1. What we are building

A small, variant-aware research atlas that helps a patient-group leader move through:

**Diagnosis → variant-specific mechanism → related research → existing asset → potential partner → a concrete research proposal.**

Working title: **GRIN research connections: a variant-aware atlas.**

The first question is:

> For selected GRIN2B and GRIN2A variants with evidence of reduced receptor function, which existing registry measures and study methods could inform a shared observational research proposal, and what must be checked before adapting them?

The output is a sourced proposal for expert review. It does not establish treatment suitability, trial eligibility or permission to access patient data.

## 2. Why this cluster

The [challenge brief](../Knowledge%20graph) asks for one complete journey from disease to an explained connection, reusable asset, collaborator and next step. It also explicitly values mechanism-aware clustering, counterexamples and honest uncertainty (physical pages 3–6).

| Reason | Why it helps our submission |
|---|---|
| Narrow biological scope | Two genes and one defined functional research question keep the first dataset small enough to curate carefully. |
| A defensible cross-gene connection | GRIN2A and GRIN2B encode NMDA-receptor subunits; published studies characterize variant-specific functional effects. We can demonstrate a connection through biology rather than name matching. |
| Real cross-gene research | A published phase 2A study included participants with loss-of-function variants in GRIN2A, GRIN2B and GRIN1. It supplies an inspectable example of research organized across genes, while its uncontrolled design limits conclusions. |
| Existing infrastructure | The GRIN Variant Patient Registry explicitly investigates differences across genes and functional effects. It gives the demo a concrete asset and an access pathway to explain. |
| Visible communities and resources | GRIN2B Foundation and CureGRIN provide public research information. GRIN2B Foundation lists models, clinical centers and a COMBINEDBrain sample-request route. |
| A strong counterexample | A variant in the same gene can have a different functional effect. The interface can explain why one research connection is included and another requires separation or further review. |

Supporting sources: [functional classification study](https://academic.oup.com/hmg/article/32/19/2857/7208877), [cross-gene study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38380699/), [registry description](https://grin2b.com/grin-registry/), [research resources](https://grin2b.com/for-researchers/) and [CureGRIN research participation](https://curegrin.org/gri-research-participation/).

This recommendation is based on source availability and fit to the challenge. We have not run a formal comparative evaluation of disease clusters or a patient usability study.

## 3. Exact scope of the first build

| Dimension | Initial boundary |
|---|---|
| Starting diagnosis | GRIN2B-related neurodevelopmental disorder |
| Connected diagnosis | GRIN2A-related neurodevelopmental disorder |
| Core research group | Selected variants with published functional characterization supporting loss of function, preserving the source's degree of certainty |
| Comparison group | Selected gain-of-function variants, displayed separately for this loss-of-function research question |
| Uncertain cases | Possible, indeterminate, conflicting or insufficiently characterized effects remain qualified or unresolved |
| Main asset | Publicly described GRIN registry infrastructure and documented study methods/outcome measures |
| Additional asset | One verified model or biorepository resource if its relevance and access route can be established |
| Primary action | Prepare a proposal to discuss shared observational measures and study methods with an appropriate research team |
| First expansion | GRIN1, after the two-gene journey works |

**Dataset targets, not current counts:** 6–10 carefully selected variants, 10–15 key papers, 2–3 assets, two relevant organizations and a few verified researcher or institutional contacts. Fewer well-supported records are acceptable if they complete the journey.

Keep the first version within GRIN2A/GRIN2B. Broader GRIN/GRIA disorders, all genetic epilepsies, comprehensive drug matching and whole-world ingestion are later scope decisions.

## 4. Scientific distinctions the graph must preserve

Cluster membership is a contextual research hypothesis, not a property assigned to every person with a particular gene.

- **Pathogenicity and functional effect are separate.** A pathogenic classification alone does not establish loss or gain of receptor function.
- **Preserve the original functional category.** The published framework distinguishes likely/possible loss of function, likely/possible gain of function, indeterminate effects and no detectable effect. Do not flatten them into a single confident binary label.
- **Record experimental context.** Retain the assay, construct/subunit context, measured property, species or model, and source interpretation when available. In-vitro receptor findings do not establish identical effects throughout the brain.
- **Keep clinical differences visible.** Similar functional direction does not mean equivalent phenotype, natural history, outcome-measure validity or response to intervention.
- **Do not turn a research exclusion into a clinical contraindication.** A gain-of-function case is outside this particular loss-of-function proposal; that is not a blanket statement about all possible shared research.
- **Retain disagreement.** Conflicting findings and untested variants should produce review questions, not forced classifications.

Use claim-level sources, supporting passages or fields, dates and review states. Extraction confidence describes extraction reliability; it must not appear as a probability of clinical success. See the [functional classification framework](https://academic.oup.com/hmg/article/32/19/2857/7208877).

## 5. Our contribution and the novelty claim

The GRIN cross-gene connection and shared registry already exist. We should explicitly acknowledge that work.

Our contribution is an understandable, traceable route through fragmented evidence: resolve the variant, explain its connection, expose limitations, identify relevant existing infrastructure and prepare a focused next research action.

The demo should test whether the atlas reduces the work needed to assess an opportunity. It must not claim to have discovered the GRIN relationship, created an existing collaboration, or proved a new treatment. A proposed asset adaptation remains an inference until reviewed.

### Competitive positioning, refined after reviewing existing tools

The current [GRIN Portal](https://grin-portal.lalresearchgroup.org/) already provides variant interpretation/classification, functional and clinical information, registry links, and downloadable variant reports. [NIH Translator](https://ncats.nih.gov/research/research-activities/translator) integrates biomedical sources to support research hypothesis generation. [GRIN2B Foundation](https://grin2b.com/for-researchers/) already publishes resources, models, clinical centers and a sample-request route. These are existing capabilities to build on, not gaps we should claim to have invented solutions for.

Our proposed differentiation is **evidence-backed assessment of a specific asset-reuse opportunity, ending in a research proposal with unresolved questions assigned to the appropriate people**. This is a product hypothesis; the source review is not an exhaustive feature audit or proof that no competing tool supports it.

The initial interface should make four things work together:

1. **Question-specific compatibility:** explain whether selected variant contexts support investigating the same observational research question, retaining scientific differences and uncertainty. Shared loss of function alone does not establish compatibility.
2. **Reuse assessment:** identify the exact existing component worth assessing, then show scientific applicability, access conditions, owner and outstanding checks separately. Rank review priorities by explicit reasons, not a clinical probability or publication count.
3. **The next missing piece:** distinguish missing functional evidence, unresolved measure validity, missing permissions and absent infrastructure. Each gap should lead to a specific question and a suitable public contact or source.
4. **A traceable handoff:** assemble an editable brief whose factual statements resolve to evidence and whose proposed actions are clearly labeled. Include the strongest reasons against the proposal as well as the rationale for it.

The graph earns its place by connecting these constraints. If a critical claim is contradicted or removed, dependent opportunities must change status and explain why. Demonstrate this with a clearly labeled scenario or test; never present an artificial edit as newly discovered biomedical evidence.

For the first build, prioritize one complete reuse assessment and a reliable blocker explanation over expanding node counts. Evidence-change tracking and revalidation can follow once the initial workflow is proven; they are not currently implemented capabilities.

## 6. Research and implementation approach

### Step 1 — Assemble a small evidence pack

Start with the functional classification paper, the cross-gene clinical study, the registry description, and official organization/resource pages. Follow their references to find the original experiments for candidate variants.

For each chosen variant, verify the gene, transcript/accession, protein and nucleotide description as available, functional findings, classification and publication. Reconcile identifiers before importing. Exact demo variants are still to be selected; do not infer their identities from similar labels.

Use MONDO/HGNC/HPO/ClinVar mappings where appropriate to establish identity and phenotype context. Their identifiers or pathogenicity labels are not substitutes for functional evidence.

### Step 2 — Curate three contrasting cases

1. **Supported research lead:** a selected GRIN2B variant and selected GRIN2A variant whose source-qualified functional evidence supports investigating the shared question.
2. **Mechanism counterexample:** a selected variant with a different functional direction that is not grouped into this loss-of-function proposal.
3. **Evidence gap:** a genuinely unresolved case, or a clearly disclosed restricted-coverage demonstration, that produces an honest next question.

Have the scientific interpretation reviewed before labeling a route as supported. Automated source-span checking is useful but does not replace that review.

### Step 3 — Verify assets and contacts

For each asset, record what exists, who maintains it, what might be reusable, its scope, public access/request route and last check date. Distinguish a registry's existence from access to its records; a publication's named assessment is not automatically a freely reusable instrument.

The registry page describes access through research agreements. Emory's CFERV database states restrictions on reproducing its data without written permission. Use appropriately reusable published evidence and link to restricted resources unless permission is obtained. Verify any model or sample's specific suitability and current availability rather than inferring these from a general resource page. [Registry access](https://grin2b.com/grin-registry/), [CFERV terms](https://med.emory.edu/departments/pharmacology-chemical-biology/programs-centers/cferv/database.html).

Use public professional/institutional contacts. A listed contact is a potential discussion route, not a confirmed collaborator. No patient-level registry data are needed for the initial demo.

### Step 4 — Populate the existing graph model

Reuse the repository's nodes, claims, evidence, sources and coverage structure. The useful path is:

```text
GRIN2B-related disorder
  → selected variant
  → source-qualified functional finding
  → shared research question
  → selected GRIN2A variant / related disorder
  → relevant registry or study method
  → asset owner / research team
  → proposed next action
```

This is an exploration sequence, not a chain of established causation. Keep reported relationships separate from the inferred research opportunity. Proposed representation changes must fit the actual schema; this outline is not a claim that every displayed step is already an implemented predicate.

Use OpenAI for source-grounded extraction, synonym reconciliation and plain-language explanations, with each generated factual statement linked to retrieved claims. Add and verify the actual integration: the existing extraction prompt alone does not satisfy the challenge's OpenAI-use requirement.

### Step 5 — Connect the frontend

Follow the [frontend strategy](frontend-strategy.md): one search, a focused evidence path, selectable relationships, an asset comparison and an editable collaboration brief. Keep sources, uncertainty and access conditions visible. Provide a useful no-route state with actual search coverage.

## 7. The demo story

Maria is a fictional patient-group leader used to explain the journey. The biomedical records in the submitted real-data demo must be sourced and clearly separated from fictional presentation details.

> Maria starts with GRIN2B. She selects a variant context from the curated dataset. The atlas explains a research connection to selected GRIN2A variants, reveals an existing registry and study approach, shows the differences that require review, and prepares a proposal for discussing shared outcome measures with the research team.

| Time | Screen/action | What the judge learns |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 seconds | Search GRIN2B; show the canonical diagnosis and selected variant context | Search resolves identity without assuming a mechanism from the gene alone |
| 8–20 seconds | Open the related GRIN2A research lead | The connection follows source-qualified biology across genes |
| 20–32 seconds | Inspect an edge and its source; contrast the opposing-effect case | Evidence and exclusions are understandable |
| 32–45 seconds | Open the registry/study-method comparison | Existing work may be useful, with explicit scope and access checks |
| 45–55 seconds | Prepare the collaboration brief | Maria leaves with a partner route, specific question and milestone |
| 55–60 seconds | Show measured or clearly labeled proposed time-to-milestone improvement | The impact claim has a defined scope and assumptions |

Keep the unresolved/no-route case one click away for live judging. If it replaces the positive journey, finish with the missing evidence and a useful plan to investigate it.

The collaboration brief should contain:

- The research question and selected variant contexts.
- The supporting path, citations and relevant disagreements.
- The specific existing component worth assessing for reuse.
- Differences in populations, outcomes and functional findings.
- Permission/access questions and a verified public contact route.
- A proposed first discussion and the evidence needed for a feasibility decision.

Suggested opening question: **“Could the existing registry measures and study methods support a functionally stratified observational comparison across these selected GRIN2A and GRIN2B cases?”**

## 8. Presentation format

Prepare a working website for live exploration, a guided interactive walkthrough and the required one-minute video. Arcade is the preferred presentation tool from our discussion; Supademo is the free alternative. Tool selection is separate from the biomedical implementation.

Capture the actual frontend after the real-data journey works. Limit the guided tour to the six moments above, preserve visible caveats, and finish with a link to explore the live atlas. A recorded tour supplements the working prototype.

## 9. The 10× milestone

Target **time to an expert-reviewable shared observational research proposal**. This includes locating relevant evidence, disambiguating variants, checking an asset and finding the appropriate contact route.

Compare manual and atlas-assisted work using the same sources and quality requirements. Measure completeness, citation support, incorrect leads, unresolved checks and reviewer time as well as speed. Report preparation time separately from access agreements, scientific review, recruitment and study follow-up.

A 10× improvement is a hypothesis until measured. We have no evidence that this prototype accelerates treatment development by 10×.

### How the time saving could happen

The working hypothesis is that repeated preparation effort can be reduced by resolving identities once, reusing source-qualified evidence, traversing bounded candidate paths, checking structured asset constraints, and generating the cited draft from those records. The researcher still evaluates the scientific proposal; the system reduces the assembly and checking burden rather than assuming that review is unnecessary.

Define the target as **10× less human effort to produce a proposal that passes the same review standard**. For example, 10 person-hours reduced to 1 person-hour would meet that target. Those figures are an illustrative target, not observed timings or estimates of competitor performance. Measure elapsed calendar time separately.

Use this evaluation before making a claim:

1. Prepare a small held-out set of GRIN questions covering a plausible reuse opportunity, an opposing-effect case and missing or contradictory evidence. Keep these separate from the rehearsed demo.
2. Compare the atlas with the best available existing workflow, including GRIN Portal, literature tools, foundation resources and AI assistance where the comparator normally uses it. Also compare against a basic search/chat interface over the same permitted corpus to test the graph's incremental value.
3. Give both workflows the same task, source access and acceptance requirements. Use matched cases and counterbalance order to reduce learning effects. A small hackathon pilot is preliminary evidence, not a general performance estimate.
4. Have an independent qualified reviewer assess citation support, mechanism interpretation, asset specificity, stated access conditions, critical omissions and usefulness of the next action. Time to an acceptable result includes corrections and reviewer effort in both workflows; an incorrect fast result does not qualify.
5. Report per-case and median human time, elapsed time, completion rate, errors and reviewer corrections. Count unsuccessful cases explicitly rather than calculating speedup only on easy successes.
6. Disclose initial curation, expert validation and maintenance effort separately. Distinguish marginal time on a new query from the total cost of producing and maintaining the dataset. A prewritten answer replayed during the demo cannot establish general 10× performance.

If the pilot shows 3×, report 3× and explain the remaining bottleneck. Even a 10× result on proposal preparation does not imply 10× faster recruitment, data access, study completion or treatment development. The submission should connect the measured preparation improvement to its downstream milestone and state the remaining dependencies.

## 10. Ready-to-demo checklist

- [ ] Select and verify the exact variants for the positive route, counterexample and unresolved case.
- [ ] Curate the initial evidence pack and stable identifiers.
- [ ] Review the mechanism interpretation and retain source-specific uncertainty.
- [ ] Verify at least one relevant asset, its maintainer and its access route.
- [ ] Verify a public researcher or institutional contact and its relevance.
- [ ] Populate and validate the real-data bundle separately from synthetic fixtures.
- [ ] Run the actual OpenAI workflow and check claim-level grounding.
- [ ] Complete search → connection → evidence → asset → proposal in the frontend.
- [ ] Verify that opposing-effect and missing-evidence cases do not become unsupported leads.
- [ ] Date the dataset and distinguish cached evidence from live retrieval.
- [ ] Test comprehension with a non-specialist and scientific interpretation with a suitable expert.
- [ ] Measure the preparation milestone, rehearse the one-minute story and record the walkthrough.
